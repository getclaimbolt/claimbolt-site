The denials we take on

We focus on the categories where denials are expensive, common and often reversible, so we know the plan rules and the arguments that work.

ABA and autism therapy

Hours cut or denied as not medically necessary, visit limits, and plans ignoring state autism coverage mandates.

Mental health and substance use care

Residential, inpatient and intensive outpatient stays denied or cut short. Federal parity law is often on your side.

Specialty drugs

Biologics, GLP-1s and other high-cost drugs denied for step therapy, formulary or off-label reasons.

Prior authorization

Surgery, imaging and procedures denied before they happen, including after a failed peer-to-peer review.

Home health

Over 78% of appealed home healthcare denials were overturned in the New York study (Healthcare Dive).

Surprise out-of-network bills

Emergency care and out-of-network providers at in-network facilities, where the No Surprises Act may protect you.

Questions

How is the 20% calculated?

It’s 20% of the money paid or the bill reduced because of the appeal. If the win is approval for ongoing care, such as weekly therapy or a monthly drug, the fee is based on the first 12 months of approved benefit, as set out in your agreement. If nothing is recovered, you owe nothing.

Practices: do you need access to our billing system?

No. We work from the denial letters, remittance advice and records you export to us through a secure link. We prepare each appeal and either submit it as your authorized representative or hand it to your team ready to send, depending on what each payer requires.

Practices: which plan should we choose?

Recovery suits a backlog or unpredictable volume, since you only pay on results. The appeals desk suits a steady flow of new denials and works out cheaper once you’re recovering more than about $7,500 a month. The estimator above shows which is cheaper for your numbers.

Can you guarantee a result?

No, and nobody honest can. We promise a careful, well-supported appeal filed on time, and that we’ll tell you upfront if we don’t think a case is winnable.

My deadline is close. Can you still help?

Probably. Put the date in the form. If waiting could seriously harm your health, you can ask for an expedited appeal, which plans have to decide quickly.

Are you a law firm?

No. ClaimBolt is not a law firm and doesn’t give legal advice. Most insurance appeals are administrative and don’t need a lawyer. If yours does, we’ll tell you.
